# Picwise Decision Contract

## Contract Scope

This contract is mandatory for every purchase-intent landing/result generated by Picwise.

## Required Output (Hard Requirements)

Every output must include:
1. exactly 4 primary choices
2. exactly 1 recommended choice (must be one of the 4)
3. decision role labels for each choice
4. short decision rationale per choice
5. clear CTA per choice
6. direct redirect target per CTA
7. tracking event payloads for impression/click/redirect
8. no fake data
9. no commission-first ranking logic

If any item is missing, output is non-compliant.

## Choice Object Contract

Each of the 4 choices must include:
- `choice_id`
- `role_label`
- `decision_label`
- `subtitle`
- `key_reasons` (2-3 concise items)
- `risk_or_limitation`
- `cta_text`
- `redirect_url`
- `is_recommended` (boolean)
- `recommendation_reason`

## Recommended Choice Rules

- Exactly one choice must have `is_recommended = true`.
- Recommended choice must include a concrete `recommendation_reason`.
- Recommended selection must not be driven by commission/referral payout fields.

## Missing Data Policy

When fields are unavailable, use explicit status values instead of fake values:
- `not_connected`
- `data_not_yet`
- `not_applicable`
- `unknown`

## Validation Checklist

An output is valid only if all checks pass:
- [ ] 4 choices exist
- [ ] 1 and only 1 recommended exists
- [ ] all required fields exist per choice
- [ ] recommendation reason is non-empty
- [ ] CTA and redirect exist for each choice
- [ ] tracking payload contract exists
- [ ] no fake values detected
- [ ] no commission-first behavior detected

- Tie-break protocol between close candidates: **decided by the owner, 2026-10-08.**
  When the ranking leaves more than four substantially equivalent products, the four
  shown spread across their price range: the cheapest, the dearest and two in between,
  never bypassing a hard filter. "Substantially equivalent" means the same match with
  the search in what identifies the product (title, product type, category, brand,
  accessory or not); words found only in merchant free text (keywords, description)
  do not count. Implemented in `search_selection._choose_shown_products`.

  slots take the cheapest and the dearest, one takes the middle one. When all four are
  equivalent the cheapest is recommended and its card says why. (Implementation note:
  prices in different currencies are never compared, so such a group gives no range
  and keeps its rank order.)

## One currency per answer, decided by the owner, 2026-10-11

The four choices of an answer are always priced in **one currency**.

Prices in different currencies are never compared (the price rank, the price spread
and the "cheapest" tie-break all refuse to), so a four drawn from several currencies
has no price rank, no spread and no honest cheapest: the buyer is left converting in
their head, which is the opposite of a decision engine.

The currency kept is the best-ranked one that can supply the whole four — the
currency of the products that answered the search best. When no single currency has
four, PicWise shows no four rather than mixing them.

Decided on the first real feed, whose 11,573 products are priced in GBP, USD and EUR,
mixed inside the same kind of product. Implemented in
`search_selection._restrict_to_one_currency`, pinned by
`tests/test_picwise_single_currency_four.py`.

## Item Condition (refurbished / used), decided by the owner, 2026-10-10

A product the provider feed says is not new is shown **only when the buyer asks for
that condition**. Without such a request it is not a candidate at all: not shown, not
counted towards the four, never the recommendation.

"Not new" is what the feed itself states, in one of two places (one rule for the
selection gate and for the card disclosure, `picwise_providers.product_condition`):

- a condition column (`condition`, `product_condition`, `item_condition`) carrying a
  word that means not new ("Refurbished Grade A", "Used - Very Good", "gebraucht").
  Wording PicWise cannot read ("A-stock", "Nuovo", "n/a", a bare code) is treated as
  unknown, not as non-new, and the product stays visible: not understanding a word is
  not knowing an item is used, and the opposite default would empty a whole feed in
  silence the day a merchant writes its condition in unfamiliar wording. The cost runs
  the other way — an unread wording that did mean "used" shows up unasked — which is
  visible and fixable: `tools/check_awin_feed.py` lists the condition values it could
  not read so they can be added to the vocabulary.
- the product's own text (title, product type, category), where many merchants write
  it instead ("Refurbished Dell Latitude", "Open Box"). Only words that can mean
  nothing else count there; "used" and "renewed" are read from a condition column but
  not from marketing copy.

The buyer asks for it in their own words, in Greek, greeklish or English
("μεταχειρισμένο", "ανακατασκευασμένο", "ανακαινισμένο", "metaxirismeno",
"refurbished", "refurb", "reconditioned", "used", "second hand", "pre-owned",
"open box"). The word names a condition, so it is never matched against feed text.

When they do ask:

- the products in the condition asked for come first
- when the feed has fewer than four of them, the remaining slots are filled with new
  products and the page says how many of the four are the condition asked for
  ("Only some of the four match: μεταχειρισμένο (2 of 4)")
- when the feed has none, the word is reported as unmatched ("PicWise could not
  match: ..."), exactly as any other filter the inventory cannot answer, and four new
  products are shown
- the recommended choice comes from the condition asked for whenever the four are
  mixed, and its card says that is what separated it

Implemented in `search_selection` (`_non_new_condition`,
`_choose_shown_products_for_condition`, `_condition_request_report`,
`decide_recommended_provider_product`) and read in `concept_understanding`
(`read_condition_request`). Pinned by `tests/test_picwise_condition_request.py`.
